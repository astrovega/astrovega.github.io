About me

I am an astrophysicist and data scientist working at the intersection of astronomy, artificial intelligence, and data-driven research.

My scientific work focuses on galaxy formation and evolution, galaxy morphology, low-surface-brightness structures, galaxy clusters, and gravitational lensing. Over the past years, I have increasingly explored how machine learning and artificial intelligence can be used to extract physical information from complex astronomical datasets.

I completed my PhD in Astrophysics at the Universidad Autónoma de Madrid and the Observatoire de Paris, studying strong gravitational lensing by galaxy clusters. I subsequently held research positions at the University of Pennsylvania, IFCA-CSIC, IIIA-CSIC, IAC, Universidad de Valladolid, and CEFCA.

I currently work as a Lead Data Scientist at Deduce Data Solutions, where I develop AI-based solutions for scientific and industrial problems while remaining involved in astrophysics research and international collaborations.

My research has involved major observational and simulation projects including Euclid, JWST/CEERS, J-PLUS and J-PAS, The Three Hundred, and the ARRAKIHS mission.

Research

My research explores how galaxies form, evolve and acquire their observed structure, combining astronomical imaging, numerical simulations and increasingly, machine-learning and computer-vision techniques.

Galaxy evolution & morphology

A major part of my work focuses on the structure and morphology of galaxies across cosmic time. I have studied galaxy morphologies in large surveys and at high redshift, including work with DES, JWST/CEERS and cosmological simulations. This includes automated morphological classification, self-supervised representation learning, galaxy sizes, stellar disks, bars and structural evolution.

The low-surface-brightness Universe

I am interested in the faint structures surrounding and shaping galaxies: stellar outskirts, disk truncations, tidal features, dwarf and satellite systems, and diffuse low-surface-brightness emission. My recent work in this area involves Euclid, J-PLUS/J-PAS and ARRAKIHS, with particular emphasis on astronomical image processing, PSF modelling and subtraction, source removal and the construction of analysis pipelines for very faint structures.

Artificial Intelligence for Astrophysics

Since 2019, machine learning has become a central component of my research. I have developed and applied methods based on convolutional neural networks, supervised and self-supervised learning, dimensionality reduction, foundation models and image segmentation. Applications have ranged from galaxy morphology and structural measurements to source detection, redshift-related problems and the analysis of astronomical detector signals.

Galaxy clusters & gravitational lensing

My earlier research focused on galaxy clusters, dark matter and gravitational lensing. I developed lens models for the Hubble Frontier Fields, studied the statistics and efficiency of strong lensing in observed and simulated clusters, and investigated applications ranging from dark-matter constraints to cosmology. This line of work includes contributions to The Three Hundred project and the use of multiply lensed supernovae for measuring the Hubble constant.
